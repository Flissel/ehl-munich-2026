Customer comment:

> overall the car is really great, and there's a lot to appreciate about it both when i'm driving and as someone who owns it. i'm obsessed with how it looks; i honestly get giddy every time i walk up to it. the build quality is the main reason i picked this instead of a model s, and as a car it's fantastic. launch mode, the light bar, all sorts of details are just awesome. but i still only give it about a 7 or 8 out of 10 because of the range, the seats, the way you can (or can't) customize things, and the cabin space.

range - i get the mindset that if it's electric then it's meant to be a commuter, but that really doesn't fit every demographic or psychographic. i work from home, so most of the time it's just driving me around town and to the airport, but the car is so addictive to drive that we're now actively planning road trips and at the same time complaining about its range. there are areas of our state, utah, that we simply can't go to because the charging infrastructure just isn't there yet.

on top of that, i'm really annoyed that you rolled out a mid-cycle efficiency update on the i5 only about four months after i bought mine.

seat comfort - i have a hard time getting comfortable in these seats. people like me with office jobs usually don't have perfect backs, so we need a way to make the seat flatter and less curved in certain spots to be comfortable. the lumbar is too aggressive by default and you can't get the seat bottom to go truly flat either. having more adjustment options would be very welcome. let the seat move to a completely neutral position first, then let us tailor things from that baseline.

cabin space - this is the most money i've ever paid for a car, and even though the i5 is about the same size as a 7-series was ten years ago, my rear passengers have less room than they did in my 2009 nissan altima. way too much space has been allocated to the trunk. even giving the back seat one extra inch of legroom would improve it. i'm 6 feet tall, and with the comfort exits turned on, when i get out or in i basically crunch whoever is sitting behind me. that's not acceptable for a car in this price range.

in-car & app customization - personal mode should really be a blend of the best parts of all the different driving modes. i want personal mode to truly mean personal. for example, i love the sounds from expression mode, but i don't want the rest of expression mode active; so why can't i pull just those sounds into personal, or bring any single setting from the other modes into personal mode? on the app side, my 2022 hyundai tucson actually offers more customization, especially around the climate controls. i get that you're aiming for a simple "one button, no thinking" climate concept, but i want the ability to build my own climate profiles: like a winter@80deg setting with seat heaters, or a summer@65 setting with the seat coolers on. the last-used climate setup isn't always what i want the car to return to when i get back in. tesla is really doing great things in their app right now; pretty much any function on the car has an api call and can be accessed directly through the app. that's the kind of direction i think you should move toward.

and one more note about expression mode: it has the best sound design overall, but the sound it makes at 'idle' is this high-pitched whirr, and that noise alone keeps me from using it regularly. if that whirr weren't there, expression mode might actually become my default setting.
