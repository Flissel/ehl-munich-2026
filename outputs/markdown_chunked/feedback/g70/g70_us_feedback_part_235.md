### G70-3138

- Source: Source A
- Vehicle: G70
- Market: US
- Engine type: ICE
- Feedback types: Defect, Difficult to Use, Wants, unknown_feedback_type
- Signal types: complaint, unknown, unmet_need

Classifications:

- Defect (complaint): (Active) cruise control : Malfunction / (Active) cruise control / Malfunction
- Difficult to Use (complaint): Drivetrain : Not user-friendly / Drivetrain / Not user-friendly
- Wants (unmet_need): Interior comfort : Customer request: comfort / Interior comfort / Customer request: comfort
- Difficult to Use (complaint): Navigation : Not user-friendly / Navigation / Not user-friendly
- Defect (complaint): Side window, open / close : Damage / Side window, open / close / Damage
- Defect (complaint): Steering : Malfunction / Steering / Malfunction
- unknown_feedback_type (unknown): no_class_found / no_class_found

Customer comment:

> while i actually prefer how the 7 series interior looks compared to the s class, i would still tell most family and friends to go with the s class instead, because of a mix of issues i've run into.

first, the premium audio: the top b&w setup is just so-so. for a "top of the line" system it doesn't stack up against the burmester premium system at all.

then the transmission behavior. when you really get on the throttle, the shifts in my 760i are noticeably rough. even my previous s63 amg, under heavy acceleration, shifted more smoothly than this car does.

the navigation software is another weak point. when driving in complicated road situations, it's not as intuitive or clear. it doesn't give enough early guidance for moving across several lanes on 6- or 8-lane city freeways, and it's not very clear about things like multi-lane exits where you need to pick a specific lane right after you exit. on top of that, the lane guidance is shown only in the hud, which feels like a bad design choice; those lane markers really should also show up on the main center display.

overall, the instrument screen behind the steering wheel is used poorly. both mercedes and audi handle that area much better, integrating it in a more sensible way with the center screen and hud, and audi especially is far ahead when it comes to how you can customize what's shown there.

the rear side window blinds are another disappointment, for two reasons. first, you have to go into the media screens to raise the shades, instead of them simply coming up automatically with a second press on the window raise hard button. second, the open channel where the side blinds sit looks unfinished. it comes across as unprofessional compared to what mercedes or audi do, where they have top covers hiding the blinds when they're stowed.

not being able to turn off auto start stop separately from the chosen drive mode is also annoying.

with the cruise control follow distance, there are two problems. you can't adjust the following gap while actually driving without digging through multiple menus, and even at the maximum setting the car follows too closely at moderate speeds. between about 45 and 60 mph, the farthest setting still feels uncomfortably, almost rudely, close to the car ahead.

the automatic lane keeping is behind the competition too. compared to audi and mercedes, it doesn't hold the lane as reliably or as smoothly, often failing to stay centered and tending to wander side to side. the lane keeping in this 760 really reminds me of mercedes' first generation system from something like 10 to 12 years ago.

### G70-3140

- Source: Source B
- Vehicle: G70
- Market: US
- Engine type: ICE
- Feedback types: Difficult to Use
- Signal types: complaint

Classifications:

- Difficult to Use (complaint): Priorities for telephones : Not user-friendly / Priorities for telephones / Not user-friendly

Customer comment:

> Customer stated he was struggling to swap from one phone to another while driving. Writer obtained RVG in order to guide the customer through the setup. Writer directed the customer to the apps menu, then to mobile devices, and pointed out that this is where he can pick a different phone, temporarily disconnect one, choose CarPlay, and similar options. Writer suggested he press and hold on mobile devices to add it to shortcuts so he would not need to dig through the full menu every time he wants to change phones. Customer commented they had been having all kinds of problems and had previously been told the only way to do it was to open his wife's phone and go into Bluetooth. Customer said this approach was a hassle and also somewhat unsafe, because while driving he should not be holding his phone and navigating into utilities. Customer indicated the shortcut made a big difference and is much simpler.
